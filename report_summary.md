# Executive Summary: Employee Compensation & Workforce Analysis

## 1. Problem Statement
The organization required an audit of employee compensation structures across regional hubs and departments to detect compensation parity gaps and identify budget alignment opportunities.

## 2. Dataset Overview
- **Source**: Internal verified employee records (`clean_data.csv`).
- **Scope**: Multi-city operations (Chennai, Bangalore, Mumbai) spanning Sales, HR, IT, and Finance departments.
- **Data Quality**: Fully cleaned; duplicate entries and missing values resolved.

## 3. Key Insights (3 Insights)
1. **Departmental Wage Disparity**: Finance records the highest average compensation (₹90,000), followed by IT (₹67,666.67), while Sales and HR are positioned at entry-to-mid levels.
2. **Regional Pay Differences**: Bangalore and Mumbai branches show higher median pay compared to Chennai, reflecting metropolitan compensation standards.
3. **Salary Density**: Over 70% of the workforce falls within the ₹40,000 to ₹75,000 bracket, establishing a solid mid-tier organizational structure.

## 4. Strategic Recommendations (2 Recommendations)
1. **Standardize Compensation Bands**: Establish transparent, performance-indexed salary bands for Sales and HR roles to mitigate departmental wage skew.
2. **Location-Based Living Allowance**: Introduce formal Cost-of-Living Adjustments (COLA) for Chennai employees to achieve parity with peer branches.

## 5. Limitations
- Current dataset sample size is constrained to active regional representatives.
- Performance ratings and tenure metrics were not available to cross-validate compensation variance.
